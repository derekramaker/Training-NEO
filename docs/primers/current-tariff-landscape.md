## Part 1: Status as of 2026-10-08

### 1.0 How to read the Part 1 tables

Status words:

- **In force:** being collected on entries now.
- **Ended:** no longer collected (may still matter for refunds, drawback or audits of past entries).
- **Suspended:** switched off until a stated date.
- **Announced:** stated by the government, or in a legal instrument with a future start date.
- **Pending:** under investigation; no duty yet.

Confidence labels (use these exactly when you update a row):

- **Confirmed:** the primary source (the legal instrument or the agency's own implementing guidance) says this. In this edition that means the official text was read in search excerpts on 2026-10-08. From your first update on, mark a row Confirmed only after you have opened the source yourself that day.
- **Reported:** known from reputable secondary sources (law firms, brokers, trade press, Congressional Research Service) or from an official summary such as a fact sheet, but not seen in the legal text. Treat it as a lead.
- **Unclear:** not corroborated, sources conflict, or the status is likely to change soon.

A row can carry two labels, for example "Confirmed (rate, dates); Reported (litigation)". Dates are written YYYY-MM-DD; times are US Eastern unless stated. A Federal Register link of the form https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2026-06960 opens the document with that number.

#### US-8 Stacking and reporting order

- **Default:** additional duties add to the base rate and to each other, unless the instrument says otherwise. Every instrument has to be read for its own "does not apply to" or interaction paragraph.
- **Section 232 metals:** full customs value from 2026-04-06. A good listed for more than one metal pays once (reported). Separate metal-content and non-metal lines apply only to entries before 2026-04-06.
- **Section 301 forced labor:** not applied to articles subject to Section 232 (CSMS 69326983). For China it adds to the existing Section 301 duties on Lists 1 to 4A (reported).
- **Section 122 (ended):** did not apply to goods subject to Section 232.
- **Section 338 on goods of Canada:** adds to the column 1 rate, AD/CVD and other duties, including the forced-labor duty where that applies (CSMS 69606660). Interaction with Section 232: at launch, goods subject to listed Section 232 duties could claim the 0% line 9903.03.15 on all three lists. For entries from 12:01 a.m. ET 2026-09-15, only goods under 9903.03.13 (the dairy list) may claim it, so goods under 9903.03.12 (the alcoholic beverage list) and 9903.03.14 (the motor vehicle list) that are also subject to Section 232 pay both (CSMS 69851916; Proclamations 11064 and 11065) (Confirmed). 9903.03.16 is a separate 0% exclusion for qualifying Canadian civil aircraft, engines and parts, not a Section 232 line (reported).
- **Partial-value bases still live:** the non-US content of Commerce-approved USMCA vehicles and trucks under Section 232, and the non-US content (plus any US content above 40% of value) of USMCA-qualifying Annex I-C equipment under the Section 232 metals regime (Proclamation 11032, to 2027-12-31).

### 1.5 USMCA, CUSMA, T-MEC: the joint reviews

What the agreement says (durable, Article 34.7, https://ustr.gov/sites/default/files/files/agreements/FTA/USMCA/Text/34_Final_Provisions.pdf):

- The agreement entered into force on 2020-07-01 for a 16-year term (to 2036-07-01).
- The Free Trade Commission holds a joint review on the sixth anniversary, 2026-07-01. Each party confirms in writing, through its head of government, whether it wants to extend the term for another 16 years.
- If all three confirm, the term is extended to 2042 and the next joint review is due within six years.
- If any party does not confirm, the agreement stays in force and the parties hold annual joint reviews for the rest of the term. Any later review can still confirm the extension. Without it, the agreement ends on 2036-07-01.

## Part 2: How to research a new tariff action

### 2.1 What you produce, and the boundary

- **Research owns the WHAT:** the legal authority, scope (HTS, Chapter 99, countries, product language), rates, effective dates, exclusions, stacking, USMCA treatment and sources. You deliver it in a Tariff-Impact Brief (`docs/templates/domain/tariff-impact-brief.md`) and the Action tab of the shared Tariff-Impact Model (`docs/templates/domain/tariff-impact-model-guide.md`).
- **Standard Cost owns the $:** landed cost, margin, the Oracle cost update and variance explanations. Standard Cost never decides scope: it computes $ on your determinations. If anyone asks you "how much will this cost us?", the answer is: "Standard Cost will model it from brief v1.0. I can tell you which items, from when and at what rate."
- **Maker-checker:** no brief is released without a documented check by the Senior or the Manager. This continues after day 90.
- **No legal advice:** you record "our working interpretation" with a confidence level. Anything uncertain that changes scope, rate or date goes to the broker or trade counsel (through the Senior, see 2.6) before release.

### 2.2 Where announcements appear, and in what order

Only the legal instrument and the agency's implementing guidance are sources. Press releases, fact sheets, social media posts, news articles, law-firm alerts and broker newsletters are leads: use them to find the instrument, and expect details to change between the announcement and the text. Fact sheets in 2026 sometimes differed from the proclamation (for example on rates and dates); the proclamation governs.

Useful starting points that are summaries, not sources: CBP's "U.S. Tariff Overview" PDF on cbp.gov (seen for January and February 2026; search cbp.gov for the latest) and the CBP trade remedies pages.

### 2.3 How a US action shows up on an entry

- HTSUS Chapter 99 holds temporary modifications. Subchapter III (headings 9903) holds the additional duties under trade laws such as Sections 232, 301, 201, 122 and 338. Subchapter II (9902) holds temporary duty reductions, which are a different thing.
- A Chapter 99 number is reported **in addition to** the Chapter 1 to 97 classification, never instead of it. It tells CBP which action applies (or which exemption or exclusion is claimed) and therefore which extra rate to charge. CBP's order-of-reporting page gives the sequence (US-8).
- Some Chapter 99 numbers mean "not subject" or "0%" (for example 9903.05.93 for Canadian goods entered free of duty under USMCA, 9903.74.06 for the US content of an approved truck, or an excluded product). Reporting them is how the exemption is claimed.
- Examples to recognise in entry data (always check the current HTSUS): 9903.80, 9903.81 and 9903.85 for steel and aluminum before 2026-04-06, and 9903.82 headings for the metals regime from 2026-04-06; 9903.88 for Section 301 on China; 9903.94 for autos and auto parts; 9903.74 for medium and heavy trucks; 9903.76 for timber and wood products; 9903.03 for Section 122 (ended 2026-07-24) and Section 338 on Canada; 9903.05 for Section 301 on Brazil and the forced-labor duties. Confidence: Reported.

### 2.6 What to confirm with the broker or trade counsel

The BSA does not give legal advice. Record who confirmed what, when and how (Brief Part 8), and file the email in [COMPANY: email archive].

**How the questions travel:** before your day-60 review you never contact a customs broker, agente aduanal or trade counsel directly. Write the questions in the brief (Part 10) and send them to the Senior (or the Manager), who asks them. After day 60, with the Senior's agreement, you may email the broker with the Senior copied. Trade counsel is always contacted through [COMPANY: who may contact trade counsel].

Questions for the licensed customs broker:

- How they will file it: Chapter 99 numbers, line splits, reporting order, from which date and time.
- What data they need from us that we do not send today (content values, metal weight, melt or smelt country, USMCA claim data).
- How they will claim in-transit relief and what proof they need.
- Whether their system is ready by the effective date, and what happens to entries near the cutoff. Holding or accelerating entries is a business decision for the Manager, not Research.

Questions for trade counsel (through [COMPANY: who may contact trade counsel]):

- Any Medium or Low interpretation that changes scope, rate or date.
- Whether to file post-summary corrections, protests or court actions to preserve refund rights.
- Origin questions involving processing in a third country (substantial transformation).
- Any proposal to change a classification because of a new duty. Classification follows the goods, never the duty rate: send these to the Senior first.

Drawback questions go to the Senior, who owns US duty drawback (M9).

### 2.9 Trainer review rubric

Score each criterion 0, 1 or 2. A **material error** is anything that would change which items pay, how much they pay or from when: a wrong or unsupported scope, rate, origin rule, effective date or time, USMCA treatment, value base or stacking result. Any $ figure in a brief is also a material error.

| # | Criterion | 2 = Meets | 1 = Partly | 0 = Material error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Primary sources | Every fact has a primary source with its date; news, fact sheets and alerts used only as leads | One or two facts cite a secondary source but are correct | A fact rests on a secondary source and is wrong, or a key source is missing |
| 2 | Authority and instrument | Statute, instrument, number, date and issuer correct | Minor citation error | Wrong authority or wrong instrument |
| 3 | Timing | Effective date, time, time zone and basis quoted exactly; in-transit and end or step dates stated | Quoted but one secondary date missing | Wrong effective date or time, or basis misread |
| 4 | HTS scope | List taken from the source as text; digit level and Chapter 99 headings correct | Small format slip with no effect on matching | Codes missing or added; wrong heading |
| 5 | Origin and value base | Correct origin rule; full value or partial-value base stated correctly | Rule correct but not explained | Wrong origin rule; full value used where a content base applies, or an old content base used where the full value applies |
| 6 | Exclusions and exemptions | Each has an identification method and the evidence needed | Identification method vague | Exclusion missed or misapplied |
| 7 | USMCA, stacking, de minimis, drawback | Each answered with a source, or "not applicable" | One answered without a source | Wrong USMCA or stacking answer |
| 8 | Interpretation and confidence | Quote then interpret; honest confidence; Medium or Low items that change scope sent to the broker or counsel through the Senior | Confidence missing on one item | A guess presented as fact |
| 9 | Item scoping | Query saved; counts add up; borderline items listed | Counts add up but the query is not saved | Counts do not reconcile, or origin taken from the supplier address |
| 10 | Boundary and control | No $; version, change log and Jira link complete; maker checklist done | One control item missing | $ figures or pricing advice in the brief |

How to use it:

- **Outline (A30-6):** score criteria 1 to 4 only. Pass: no 0 and at least 6 of 8, with the sources found within 60 minutes.
- **Full brief (A60-1):** pass means no 0 (no material error) and at least 16 of 20.
- **Proficiency:** two full briefs in a row with no 0 and only light edits support a rating of 3 (Independent) for "find and read primary sources" (A60-6).
- The checker records the scores, findings and decision on the Jira story and in Brief Part 12.
